# Reflection

Completing the Multi-Cloud Explorer mission gave me a better understanding of how cloud platforms work and how they can be selected based on different needs. Among AWS, Microsoft Azure, and Google Cloud Platform, **AWS impressed me the most** because of its wide range of services, flexibility, and ability to support different types of organizations. I also found Azure and GCP impressive because of their strengths in Microsoft technologies, artificial intelligence, data analytics, and Kubernetes.

AWS, Azure, and GCP have many similarities. All three provide cloud-based computing, storage, networking, databases, identity and access management, and virtual machine services. They also allow organizations to scale resources based on their requirements instead of depending only on physical servers. For example, a Linux server can be migrated to **Amazon EC2**, **Azure Virtual Machines**, or **Google Compute Engine**.

However, business requirements greatly influence cloud platform selection. Organizations need to consider their budget, existing technologies, workload, scalability, security, performance, and future goals. A university that already uses Microsoft technologies may benefit more from Azure, while an AI research organization may prefer GCP because of its AI and data capabilities. A startup or global business may choose AWS because of its broad service portfolio and flexibility.

Acting as a **Cloud Solutions Consultant** taught me that choosing a cloud platform is not simply about identifying which provider is the best. It is about understanding the client's situation and matching the right services to their actual requirements. The decision matrix and client scenarios helped me practice making technology recommendations based on business needs.

Finally, my **GitHub Cloud Computing Portfolio** has improved significantly after completing this mission. It now contains organized research, platform comparisons, client recommendations, Linux investigation results, and reflections. Instead of simply storing files, the portfolio now demonstrates my ability to research cloud technologies, analyze options, document technical findings, and make practical cloud recommendations.
